# Peer Review Report

**Manuscript (proposed):** "Coalitional Game Theory for Explainable Graph-Based Recommendation Systems: A Survey and Taxonomy"
**Materials reviewed:** `spec.md`, `Paper_Structure.md`, `Implementation_Spec.md`, `CoalGameRec_Analysis.md`
**Target venue:** *Discover Artificial Intelligence* (Springer Nature), article type **Review**
**Reviewer role:** Senior reviewer, Discover AI (XAI / ML / Recommender Systems / GNNs / Cooperative Game Theory)

---

## 0. Preliminary note on what was actually submitted

Before evaluating content, a procedural point has to be flagged: **none of the four files is a manuscript.** They are a scope specification, a section-by-section blueprint with placeholder prose instructions ("Open in the survey voice…", "State the three structural reasons…"), a technical implementation plan whose result tables are explicitly labeled *pre-registered predictions, not results*, and an internal feasibility memo. There is no Introduction, no reviewed literature, no actual comparison table, no real number. A journal reviewer cannot assess novelty, correctness, writing quality, or evidentiary support from a plan for a paper — only from the paper. My review below therefore does two things: (1) it evaluates the plan on its own terms (is this a sound design that, if executed exactly as written, would produce a publishable Review article?), and (2) it flags several issues that will remain live problems in the actual manuscript no matter how well it is executed, because they are baked into the design.

**Recommendation at this stage: Major Revision required before this can be considered a submission at all** — i.e., this is pre-submission feedback, not a decision on a paper. Several of the points below (§1, §2) are blocking.

## 1. Major concerns

### 1.1 The "registered predictions" table is a serious research-integrity risk as currently designed
`Implementation_Spec.md` §B.1a presents Tables A and B with mean values to three significant figures (e.g., NDCG@5 = 0.093, Recall@20 = 0.194), bolded "winners," and a companion narrative ("Highlight that `shapley-mc` ranks 1st on both metrics, with the largest margin on sparse Amazon-Book") — all before a single experiment has been run. The document does self-label this as pre-registration, which is good scientific practice in principle. But the practice, as instantiated here, is dangerous:
- The tables are already typeset in the exact camera-ready format the paper will use (mean ± std placeholders, bold best-in-column), which creates a real risk that a placeholder becomes the submitted number if the pipeline is late, breaks, or partially fails — the failure mode is not hypothetical, it is one copy-paste away.
- The abstract in `Paper_Structure.md` already asserts the predicted outcome as fact ("Shapley-based attribution ranks first on both metrics across datasets and backbones, with the largest margin in the sparse regime") — this is written as a *result*, not a *hypothesis*, in a document dated the same day as the "registered prediction" file. That ordering (conclusion drafted before data exists) is precisely the pattern that pre-registration is supposed to guard against, and here it appears inside the pre-registration package itself.
- Recommendation: keep the falsification table (§B.6) — that part is good practice — but strip all numeric point-predictions down to directional/qualitative claims only ("we expect X > Y"), remove the fully formatted LaTeX-ready tables from the planning document, and add an explicit, dated, immutable pre-registration artifact (e.g., a signed OSF/Zenodo timestamp) separate from the paper's drafting files, so reviewers and co-authors can verify the prediction was not edited post hoc.

### 1.2 Fitness for the "Review" article type is doubtful given what is actually planned
Discover AI's Review type is described (correctly, in `spec.md`) as being for "critical accounts and comprehensive surveys." What is planned here is a survey **plus a full 2×2-backbone×dataset, 6-attribution-family factorial benchmark with 5 seeds, paired significance testing, and a headline results section** that the authors themselves repeatedly call "the paper's headline" and "the number reviewers will look at first" (`Implementation_Spec.md` §B.1a, §B.2). That is a primary research contribution, not incidental grounding. A handling editor doing the "technical + suitability checks" mentioned in §1.1 of `spec.md` may reasonably query why a new, extensively powered empirical study is being routed through the Review track rather than submitted as a Research article (or split into two papers). The manuscript plan does not currently address this risk anywhere except by asserting the mismatch is "resolved" (`spec.md` §11, risk register) — it is asserted, not resolved. I'd want to see an explicit paragraph in the cover letter and Introduction justifying the hybrid format, and I would not be surprised if the editor requests reclassification.

### 1.3 The flagship case study has an unresolved reproducibility problem, and the mitigation is circular
DyHuCoG — the author's own prior work — is repeatedly named as the taxonomy's worked example (`Paper_Structure.md` §4.6) and as one of the two benchmark backbones. The documents themselves report that an internal audit found **63 extraction gaps that make faithful DyHuCoG reimplementation impossible** (undefined hypergraph construction, unspecified similarity functions, dimensionally inconsistent propagation equations — `Implementation_Spec.md` §A.4). The proposed fix is either (a) "fix and pin" the construction/similarity/propagation choices and document them in this paper, or (b) swap in an independently documented hypergraph GNN.
- Option (a) is concerning for a *survey*: if the propagation equations were never fully specified in the original DyHuCoG publication, then "fixing" them now, inside a different paper, effectively invents a new version of the method and presents it as the flagship worked example of the taxonomy the reader is meant to trust as representative of published work. That should be disclosed prominently, not folded into a methods footnote.
- Option (b) is safer methodologically but undercuts the stated continuity goal ("so the survey reads as a continuation of the group's experimental line," `Implementation_Spec.md` §A.0) and means the "worked example" in §4.6 (which is explicitly DyHuCoG) is decoupled from what the benchmark actually runs — a disconnect a careful reviewer will notice between the taxonomy narrative and the empirical section.
- Either way, a paper whose own internal audit trail records that its central case study cannot be faithfully reproduced needs a much more candid treatment than "describe DyHuCoG accurately and cite its caveat" (repeated verbatim across all four documents without elaboration). I would want at least a short, explicit paragraph quantifying what changed and why the result is still informative.

### 1.4 Self-citation, self-promotion, and conflict-of-interest optics
The stated *raison d'être* of this survey, repeated in all four documents, is to "create the citation home" for four of the author's own planned method papers (ActionShap, FairShap, SignalShap, MHyperShap), to be "the first" in a self-referential portfolio, and to keep "the whole portfolio in one Q1 venue." This is stated as a strategic goal for the *authors*, which is understandable as private planning, but if it shows through in the manuscript's framing (e.g., an open-challenges section in §8 that maps one-to-one onto four not-yet-published papers by the same group, as currently planned) a reviewer will read it as promotional rather than a field-wide research agenda. Discover AI's editorial policy on this is not exotic — COPE guidance on excessive self-citation and citation stacking applies regardless of venue. Recommendation: the research-agenda section should be argued from the literature's own stated gaps (independently citable), with the author's planned work appearing as *one* possible direction among several from independent groups, not as the section's organizing structure.

### 1.5 Dual-publication / text-recycling risk is real and only partially mitigated
The plan explicitly reuses the thesis's Ch. 2.8 notation, Ch. 2/3 background and related-work material, and the DyHuCoG value-function equations, with instructions to "rewrite" rather than copy. This is the right instinct, but "do not copy verbatim" is not, by itself, a text-recycling policy. Springer Nature (and COPE) require that **overlapping prior work be explicitly cited and disclosed even when reworded**, and that equations reused from a prior publication (even paraphrased) still carry an attribution obligation if the underlying formulation is unchanged (Axis-6 notation, the `v(S) = α·NDCG + β·Diversity + γ·Context` value function is lifted directly from DyHuCoG's Eq. 1–2). The current plan mentions disclosure "in the cover letter if asked" (`spec.md` §1.1) — that is backwards; if the overlap is substantive, it belongs in the manuscript's own text (e.g., "we adopt the value function of [DyHuCoG citation], Eq. X"), not conditionally disclosed to an editor only on request.

### 1.6 Novelty claim rests on an informal, undated literature scan, not the survey's own protocol
The "no dedicated survey exists" claim — the paper's core novelty argument — is currently supported only by a short paragraph of hand-picked contrasts (`spec.md` §1.1, `CoalGameRec_Analysis.md` §3), not by the PRISMA-style protocol the paper itself proposes to use for its content. I ran a spot check on the two comparison surveys named (Zhao, Liu & Parilina, *Dynamic Games and Applications*, 2025; Li, Sun, Huang & Chen, *Autonomous Intelligent Systems*, 2024) and both exist and are indeed feature/classifier-centric, so the citations are not fabricated. However, this same search surfaced a closely related, unmentioned tutorial/survey — Jia et al., *A Comprehensive Study of Shapley Value in Data Analytics* (VLDB 2024/2025) — which covers Shapley-based **data valuation**, a player-type this taxonomy explicitly includes (Axis 1: "data tuples"). If the authors' own literature scan missed this, it is a warning sign that the "gap" claim needs the systematic search run *before* the novelty claim is finalized, not asserted first and backfilled by the search later, which is the order the build plan currently follows (`spec.md` §12 step 1 locks the taxonomy before step 2 runs the search).

### 1.7 Conflation of "attribution improves accuracy" with "attribution is a valid explanation"
This is a conceptual weakness that will persist into the finished paper regardless of how well the benchmark is executed. The benchmark's actual test (§A.7, re-ranking) measures whether Shapley-derived weights, used as a re-ranking signal, improve NDCG/Recall over uniform/attention/heuristic weighting. That is a test of whether the *derived numbers are useful features for ranking* — it is not a test of faithfulness, sufficiency, or any other explanation-quality criterion, and it cannot by itself answer SRQ3 ("what does the game-theoretic lens genuinely add... where is it relabeled reweighting?"), which is exactly the axis the benchmark is billed as answering (`Paper_Structure.md` §7.3: "the Recall@K/NDCG@K ordering across families is the empirical claim that answers... SRQ3"). If `shapley-mc` beats `attention`, that shows Shapley weights are a better re-ranking heuristic in this setup — a finding fully consistent with "the game-theoretic framing buys nothing beyond a good reweighting scheme," which is the very degeneration the paper wants to diagnose. The manuscript needs an explicit paragraph distinguishing the accuracy benchmark from any faithfulness/explanation-quality claim, or it will beg its own central question.

### 1.8 Statistical design is under-specified in a way that changes the answer
§7.5/§A.8 lists paired t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Holm–Bonferroni, Cohen's d_z, and 95% CIs, but never states the unit of pairing. Is the pairing per-user (n = number of users, comparing per-user NDCG/Recall between two families on the same split) or per-seed (n = 5)? These give very different degrees of freedom and very different Type-I error behavior, and the two are conflated across documents ("Runs: 5 seeds... report mean ± std" in §7.3 vs. "Per-user paired t-test" in §7.5). With n = 5 seeds, a paired t-test has minimal power and normality is essentially unverifiable; with per-user pairing, the "seeds" figure is irrelevant to the reported significance and only affects the mean ± std summary. The manuscript must specify, for every reported significance test, exactly which axis is the sample.

### 1.9 Fixed value-function weights undermine the "value-function-arbitrariness critique" the paper itself raises
§4.2/§6.2 promises a critical discussion of how the coalition value function `v(S)` is arbitrary and cannot be corrected for by the allocation rule — a good and important point. But the benchmark then fixes `α=0.60, β=0.25, γ=0.15, λ_pref=0.20` as defaults reused from the thesis, applies them identically to a dense (MovieLens) and a sparse (Amazon-Book) regime, and only "reports sensitivity... in the paper" as an afterthought (§A.5). If the headline result (`shapley-mc` best on both datasets) depends on weights tuned on one prior dataset and never re-tuned for the new regime, that is exactly the arbitrariness critique applied to the paper's own benchmark, and the "critical analysis" section will read as inconsistent unless the sensitivity analysis is elevated from an afterthought to a required robustness check with its own reported result.

## 2. Design and scope concerns (moderate)

- **"Small, illustrative benchmark" vs. actual scope.** Every document insists the benchmark must stay "small" and non-competitive with the planned method papers, yet it specifies 6 attribution families × 2 backbones × 2 datasets × 5 seeds × 6 cutoffs × ≥2 statistical tests — a full factorial comparative study. This is internally inconsistent: it cannot simultaneously be "illustrative" and be "the number reviewers will look at first." Pick one framing and align the surrounding text (word budget, figure/table count, abstract emphasis) to it.
- **Amazon-Book provenance weakens comparability.** Rebuilding Amazon-Book from the raw 2018 corpus (rather than the canonical 52,643-user LightGCN/HCCF split) is defensible for the temporal protocol, but it means none of the reported numbers can be checked against the extensive existing literature that uses the canonical split — which is precisely why the authors themselves mark the Amazon-Book predictions "low confidence" throughout Part B. A reviewer will ask why the survey's headline comparative claim rests on the noisier of its two datasets. Consider also reporting the canonical split's numbers as a secondary sanity check, even if the main claims use the rebuilt split.
- **Missing adjacent literature.** The taxonomy's Axis-1 "features as players" and "data tuples as players" categories are under-populated relative to the mature data-valuation literature (Ghorbani & Zou-style Shapley data valuation, DIG-FL/Shapley-in-federated-learning lines, and the VLDB tutorial surfaced above). Counterfactual/causal explanation methods for recommendation (e.g., counterfactual explanation RS lines, knowledge-graph path-based explanation methods) are not mentioned at all, even though the "adjacent, not core" carve-out (§3, out-of-scope) explicitly anticipates needing to justify exclusions — currently no such literature is even named to be excluded.
- **Abstract length.** The draft abstract in `Paper_Structure.md` (§ABSTRACT) runs well past a typical 250-word ceiling — by my count roughly 300+ words — while the venue requires **< 250 words**. This needs trimming before submission; flagging now so it isn't missed at the final compilation step.
- **APC figure should be reverified.** `spec.md` states the APC as US$1,690/€1,390/£1,190; independent aggregator sources return different current figures (e.g., ~£990–£1,140 depending on source and update date). The spec itself flags "confirm current APC at submission," which is the right caution — I'd just underline that this number has visibly moved between sources and should be pulled from the live Springer page at the time of submission, not from this spec.
- **CRediT statement reused verbatim across two different papers.** The same author-contribution split (Louhichi: conceptualization/methodology/software/writing; Nesmaoui: software/data/validation; Lazaar: supervision/analysis) is proposed for both DyHuCoG and this survey. CRediT statements should reflect what each author actually did *for this manuscript*; if a survey has different authorship dynamics (e.g., more writing-heavy, less "software," since the benchmark code is largely reused), the statement should say so rather than being copy-pasted.
- **Word/figure/table count inconsistency.** `spec.md` §9 targets "~9,000–12,000 words, 6–8 figures, 4–6 tables"; `Paper_Structure.md` targets "≈10,000–12,000 words, 7–9 figures, 5–7 tables." Minor, but should be reconciled before the build order locks section lengths.

## 3. Points in favor of the plan (for balance)

- The five-axis taxonomy (player set · value function · solution concept · role of attribution · graph structure) is a genuinely useful organizing device and, if populated with real citations, would plausibly fill a real gap — the contrast with feature/classifier-centric Shapley-XAI surveys and node/edge-classification GNN-explainer surveys is accurate as far as I can verify.
- The PRISMA-style protocol, explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria, and data-extraction form (§4 of `spec.md`) are good systematic-review practice and above the bar most "survey" papers in this space actually clear.
- The falsification/contingency table (`Implementation_Spec.md` §B.6) is a genuinely good practice — pre-committing to what a negative result would mean, and what the fallback is, is more rigorous than most empirical sections in comparable papers. My concern in §1.1 above is about the *numeric point-predictions and pre-formatted tables* sitting alongside this, not about the falsification logic itself.
- The five-seed, paired-statistics protocol (once the pairing unit is clarified, §1.8) is appropriate for a "grounding" benchmark of this size.

## 5. Summary recommendation

As a plan, this is unusually well specified for risk-awareness (the risk register, falsification table, and reproducibility caveats are better than average). But three things need to be resolved **before** drafting begins, because they are structural rather than cosmetic:

1. Remove the fully-formatted, three-significant-figure "predicted" result tables from circulation, or clearly watermark every instance (not just the header) as fictional/placeholder, given the abstract already states the predicted outcome as fact.
2. Resolve, on paper, whether this is a Review or a Research submission — the current plan's empirical scope argues for the latter.
3. Decide how DyHuCoG will be handled as the flagship example given its own audited reproducibility failure, and make that decision (and its consequences for §4.6 and the benchmark) internally consistent.

Once those are settled, the taxonomy and survey protocol are a sound basis for a genuinely useful Review article. I would want to see an actual draft — with real citations, a completed PRISMA count, and either real or clearly-marked-hypothetical benchmark numbers — before a substantive content review of the survey's arguments is possible.
